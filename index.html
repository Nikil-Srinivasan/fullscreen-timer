About & FAQ ↓

A timer you can read from the back of the room

Most web timers cap how large their digits can grow. Open one on a lecture-hall projector or a meeting-room TV and the numbers stop at some fixed size, marooned in the middle of an enormous screen. This one has no maximum: the digits are drawn as vector text fitted to the space they are given, so they fill a 5-inch phone and an 8K wall display equally well. There is nothing to configure for it to happen — open the page on any screen and it is already the right size.

Countdown or stopwatch

Set a countdown when you have a fixed slot: a conference talk, a lightning round, an exam, a workshop exercise. Type the length, pick one of the presets, or hold an arrow key. Once it is running you can still add or remove time without stopping the clock — useful when a session overruns and you decide to give it two more minutes. Switch to stopwatch mode when you want to know how long something actually took.

When a countdown reaches zero it keeps going, counting up with a minus sign, so you always know how far over you are rather than just that you are over. Before that, the digits turn amber and then red at thresholds you set, so the room gets a warning without anyone having to say anything.

Hide the timer and glance instead of stare

A timer you can watch is a timer you will watch. Hidden mode blanks the screen while the clock keeps running and flashes the time for a few seconds each minute, or every five minutes, then blanks again. Speakers stop clock-watching, and the audience is not distracted by digits ticking down throughout the talk. A tap peeks at the time whenever you want, without pausing anything or disturbing the schedule.

Built for the room, not the desk

  • Fullscreen with one key, and the controls fade away once you stop moving the mouse.
  • The screen stays awake while the timer runs, so your laptop does not sleep mid-talk.
  • Dark and light themes, following your system by default — dark for a darkened room, light for a bright one.
  • It works offline once loaded, which matters on conference wifi.
  • Shareable links: the address bar always describes the current timer, so you can send a colleague the exact setup.
  • Keyboard and scroll wheel: space to start, R to reset, arrows or the wheel to change the length.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. It is free, open source under the MIT licence, has no accounts, no adverts and no tracking.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit the timer is stored in your browser and runs without a connection. You can also install it as an app from your browser menu.

Can I use it on a projector or a TV?

That is what it is for. The digits scale to whatever screen you put them on, with no upper limit, so a 4K display shows 4K-sized numbers rather than a small timer in the middle of a large screen.

How do I hide the timer and show it every five minutes?

Press H until it reads “5 min”, or open Settings and choose it under “Hide the timer”. The screen goes blank and the time appears for a few seconds at every five-minute mark. You can change how long each flash lasts.

Will it stop my screen going to sleep?

Yes, while the timer is running, in browsers that support screen wake locks. Safari does not, so on an iPhone or iPad you may still want to raise your auto-lock time.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, on any modern browser. On iPhone and iPad, fullscreen hides the page controls but not Safari’s own toolbars, because Safari does not offer the fullscreen API to ordinary web pages.

Can I save a timer I use often?

Bookmark it. The address bar always reflects the current setup, so the bookmark reopens that exact timer, and your settings are remembered between visits anyway.
